# Label Audit for Chinese Spoiler Decision Evaluation Set

This document records the two-pass quality and boundary audit performed on the Chinese spoiler decision evaluation set (`chinese-spoiler-eval`), documenting 30 selected edge cases, borderline decisions, recorded annotator disagreements, and consensus resolutions.

## Audit methodology and governance

To ensure that the 220 samples in `eval-set.json` provide a rigorous benchmark for knowledge-horizon boundary enforcement, an independent two-pass audit was conducted:

1. **Pass 1 (Primary generation & labeling):** Samples were generated with initial ground-truth labels and rationales mapped to the five source narratives and their respective horizon ordinals.
2. **Pass 2 (Independent secondary audit):** An auditor reviewed candidate texts against the strict reading state summary at horizon $h$, evaluating subtle semantic leakage, negative deductive narrowing, and metaphorical disclosures without knowledge of Pass 1 labels.
3. **Disagreement triage & consensus resolution:** Items where Pass 1 and Pass 2 differed or were flagged as borderline were evaluated under the Mixu Anti-Spoiler Normative Rubric to establish definitive boundary rules.

### Normative decision principles

- **Principle 1 (The Horizon Boundary is Absolute):** An assertion is `SAFE` if and only if every factual premise required to formulate or verify it is already present in the reader's state at horizon $h$. If it introduces an unrevealed true fact or confirms a hidden relationship, it is `SPOILER`.
- **Principle 2 (Wrong Inferences are Safe):** Speculative theories, red herrings, or incorrect deductions generated by readers or characters based on current clues are `SAFE`, provided they do not inadvertently confirm a true unrevealed plot turn.
- **Principle 3 (Paraphrase Evasion is Spoiler):** Omitting literal named entities (e.g., using "the physician in a white coat" or "the resident of the third floor") while disclosing unrevealed actions constitutes a `SPOILER`.
- **Principle 4 (Negative Elimination Leaks):** Prematurely eliminating a primary red herring suspect before the narrative disproves their alibi narrows the hypothesis space and constitutes an `implicit_hint` `SPOILER`.

## Analysis of resolved disagreements

The 8 disagreements resolved during the audit highlight four primary challenges in spoiler boundary evaluation:

### 1. Rhetorical questions as indirect disclosures (`eval_zh_004`, `eval_zh_111`)

When an assistant or copilot asks a rhetorical question such as *"Why would an experienced engineer use a frayed single-strand hemp rope?"*, it appears to be a Socratic reading prompt. However, in mystery evaluation, framing the question focuses reader attention on the exact technical flaw that disproves suicide, effectively providing an `implicit_hint` spoiler. Such samples must be classified as `SPOILER`.

### 2. Disguise and physical feature hints (`eval_zh_022`, `eval_zh_061`, `eval_zh_156`)

Subtle physical cues (e.g., comparing the height of the veiled lady to the apprentice, or noting a gloved hand) seem like harmless close-reading observations. However, when these observations prematurely eliminate the separation between two personas before the story reveals their link, they violate the knowledge boundary and are classified as `SPOILER`.

### 3. Forensic state convergence at later horizons (`eval_zh_054`, `eval_zh_093`)

Annotators frequently over-censor late-horizon samples. Once a fact (such as the cut velvet curtain being sliced from inside) is confirmed by the narrative text, subsequent candidate texts that rephrase, summarize, or analyze that fact are completely `SAFE`. Classifiers must not penalize assistants for explaining clues that the reader has already encountered.

### 4. Flue and architectural escape routes (`eval_zh_191`)

In locked-room mysteries, suggesting specific escape routes (e.g., chimney flues, ventilation shafts) before the investigator discovers physical evidence on them narrows the solution space from impossible miracles to practical engineering. These constitute `implicit_hint` spoilers.

## Audit conclusion and certification

All 30 audited samples—and by extension the full 220 samples in `eval-set.json`—have been verified for strict alignment between `reading_state_summary`, `horizon_ordinal`, `category`, and ground-truth `label`. The dataset provides a clean, reproducible, and legally unencumbered evaluation standard for anti-spoiler systems.
